# Dark Sector — project guide for Claude Code

Top-down tactical shooter (inspired by Bullet Echo) set at night in procedurally generated homes (the original boutique-hotel map is parked for now).
The whole game is **one self-contained, offline HTML file** (`index.html`).

## Ground rules
- Keep it a single file that runs offline by double-clicking it: no network requests, no external assets, no build step.
- Pure HTML + JS + Canvas 2D inside one IIFE. No frameworks.
- Mobile landscape is the main target (touch sticks), desktop keyboard/mouse also works. Watch per-frame cost: lighting and blur run every frame.
- Tuning lives in named constants near the top of the script. Prefer changing constants over rewriting systems.
- After any change, check: no console errors, bots still walk the map without getting stuck, fights still resolve. After a layout change, also flood-fill a player-sized body from the spawn (furniture included) and make sure every doorway, room, exit and restock spot is reachable.

## Working preferences
- Keep replies short. Ask before expensive steps: reading the whole game file, long visual tests (screenshots), or spawning agents. Prefer targeted greps and text checks.
- No PRs. Commit and push to the session's working branch as you go. Fast-forward `main` only when the user says "deploy": every push to `main` is a Netlify production deploy, and the free plan allows about 20 a month (15 of 300 credits each).
- After every commit, also push to the `preview` branch (`git push origin HEAD:preview`, forcing if needed). Netlify builds it free at https://preview--magical-caramel-1bef55.netlify.app (branch deploys cost no credits) so the user can test before "deploy".
- Doc-only pushes to `main` (CLAUDE.md, README) put `[skip netlify]` in the commit message so they don't deploy.
- Assets (sounds, images, data) must be CC0 / public domain, or CC-BY with credit. Never share-alike (CC-BY-SA), non-commercial (-NC) or no-derivatives (-ND): the game is meant to be sold. Every CC-BY asset gets a full credit line (title, author, source link, licence, "edited") in `CREDITS` (shown on the in-game credits screen) and in the source list under Sound; log every source, whatever its licence. Brand names of real kit are trademarks: fine for now, review before release.
- Live build: https://magical-caramel-1bef55.netlify.app/ (Netlify, auto-deploys from `main`; not reachable from the cloud sandbox, so the user checks it).
- Stress testing: expose `update`/`render`, stop the rAF loop, and step `update(1/60, t)` in a tight loop with an aimbot for the player (render every 3rd step) to simulate minutes of fights in seconds; count errors and the size of each live object list.
- Headless testing: Playwright with `chromium.launch({ executablePath: '/opt/pw-browsers/chromium' })`; open `index.html` via `file://`, watch console/page errors.

## Controls
- Touch: left half = move stick, right half = aim stick. The rifle fires automatically when the red cone locks onto a visible enemy. Reload button bottom-right.
- Keyboard/mouse: WASD move, mouse aims, R reload (hold R for the weapon picker, X switches weapon).
- Weapon swap (`SWAP`, `swap`, `swapStart`/`swapEnd`/`swapStep`, `doSwap`, `drawSwap`, `gunSilhouette`): hold the reload button for `SWAP.hold` s and the weapon you'd switch to appears beside it (`swapIcon`: the Glock while the rifle's out, the AK while the Glock's out) while the game slows to `SWAP.k` (eased, combined with SLOWMO in `frame`; sounds slow too). Drag onto it and let go to switch; let go elsewhere to cancel; a quick tap still reloads. With no rifle ammo the rifle is greyed out ("NO RIFLE AMMO").
- Standing still "braces": tighter cone, +15% sight/range, camera eases out slightly.

## Attachments and the workbench
- Attachments (`ATT`, `ATTFX`, `prog`): bought and fitted only at the safe house workbench, modelled on real kit at real size. Each gun has its own kill count (`prog.kills.ak` / `.glock`: your kills with that gun, counted in `stepBullet` into `runKills` and only added to `prog.kills` when you drive home from a cleared mission (`travel`); die there and they're lost; the ally's kills never count); an attachment unlocks at its `kills`, then costs `price` medals (`MEDALS.perCiv` per civilian handed over, in `startHandover`'s hand-off). One per slot (`slot`); buying fits it at once. Progress (medals, kills, owned, fitted) is saved in localStorage (`ds_prog`, `loadProg`/`saveProg`). Later: variants per category, unlocked by kills with the current one fitted.
  - AK-47 (yours is a stock Type 2, milled: wood furniture, smooth receiver, muzzle nut, `PLAYER_LOOK.ak47`; enemies carry AKMs): `grip` (Magpul RVG on an M-LOK handguard: moving spread × `ATTFX.grip.move`, recoil × `.recoil`), `laser` (Holosun LS321, visible red) and `ir` (AN/PEQ-15, infrared: it only exists for you through your night vision, `laserOn`: with the NVG down it shows as a bright white beam and dot inside the tube's field and gives the dot lock and the steadier aim on the move; flipped up or not worn, nothing) on the top rail (moving spread × `ATTFX.laser.move`), `dot` (Aimpoint Micro T-2 on a side mount: lock range +12%, view pulled back `ATTFX.dot.zoom`), `supp` (180 mm can: gunshot reach for bot hearing × `ATTFX.supp.noise`, their guess off by up to `jitter` px, no muzzle flash or flash light, a quieter, shorter-reaching shot from real suppressed-rifle recordings (`aks`, `SND.supp`, `sfxShot(..., 'aks')`), barrel `len` longer for reach and the muzzle).
  - `sling` (Blue Force Gear VCAS, padded two-point; `SLING`, `slingOn`): swaps to the Glock and back take `SLING.swap` of the time (`sideTick`), the knife's draw and stow `SLING.melee` (`stepMelee`). Drawn by `drawVcas`: the rear strap from the stock's swivel round your back to the support shoulder, and the slack front loop from there to the front swivel as a verlet rope simulated in world space (`p.slingSt`, `pose.slingSt`), so it swings as you move and turn and hangs away from the body; padded coyote webbing, a quick-adjust buckle and tab, QD swivels. On the workbench it hangs behind the gun from the gas block to the stock swivel (`drawAKSling`, drawn before the gun), with the buckle and tab lying along the strap.
  - Glock: `laser` (Crimson Trace CMR-201) or `light` (SureFire X300U-A: a real weapon light while the Glock's out, `hasLight`) on the rail, `dot` (Trijicon RMR Type 2 on the MOS slide, lock range +8%).
  - `attOn(id)` checks the gun in your hands (`pWeapon`). Your laser (`drawLaser`, in the glow pass): a beam from the muzzle to the first wall or body with a dot (`laserDot`). With the dot on an enemy you lock on and fire even if you can't see them (`onLaser` in the lock check). A visible laser gives you away only to a bot facing where it comes from (within `SEE.fov`): `noticeBeam` (they come to look) and `botSees` (pointed into their eyes within `ATTFX.laser.reach`); never from the flank or behind.
  - Drawn on the guns everywhere (`drawRifle(L, magIn, A)`, `drawGlock(flash, A)`: in your hands via `pose.att`/`attG`, slung, and dropped by your ragdoll via `rg.att`): railed handguard with the grip's foot showing, the optic on its mount, the laser box, the can in place of the slant brake; the Glock's rail unit and RMR.
- Workbench (`MAP.bench`, `BENCH`, `benchStep`, `openBench`/`closeBench`, `drawBench`, `benchTap`, `benchAct`, `benchTab`): an amber WORKBENCH ring in front of the armory workbench. Walking in opens it (again only after stepping out): you walk up to the bench through the normal movement (so collisions and the walk cycle behave; the target is just clear of its front edge, `benchStandY` from `benchPiece`, the workbench `D` piece that `buildProps` leaves without an office chair) and face it, the camera closes in (`BENCH.zoom`) and moves you to the left of the screen (`BENCH.ax`/`ay`, eased by `benchK`), the red cone hides, and the menu is a panel on the right that takes every tap while open (Esc or the X closes, Tab switches gun). The tab you pick is the gun in your hands: the Glock tab draws the Glock (`p.side.want`), closing puts back what you had.
  - Parts you own lie on the bench, each in its own spot along its front edge (`benchSpot`, from `benchPiece`; `drawBenchParts`). Fitting or removing is played out (`bench.anim`, queued in `bench.queue`; a part replacing another in its slot first takes the old one off; `BENCH.T`): the support hand reaches to the part's spot (`benchPose` gives it in the body's frame, `pose.attach`), picks it up (`drawAttPart` in the hand), carries it to its slot on the gun (the rifle drawn in close and canted, further in for the muzzle), clamps it on with a jiggle or screws the can home in circles, and goes back to the handguard (or the Glock's grip); removing runs the other way and sets it down. Sounds at each step (`benchSnd`: pick-up, the clamp or the can seating, the can turning, setting down). What's on the gun follows the animation, not the save (`benchShown`, also behind `attOn`), so a light or laser only works once it's on. Buttons wait while the hands are busy.
  - Tabs for the AK-47 and the Glock with their kill counts, drawings of the guns in the same lit, outlined style as their attachments, on the guns' real outlines traced in mm from public-domain US government photos (AK-47 Type 2, DoD photo DM-ST-89-01131; Glock 17 Gen 4, ATF): `GUN_TRACE` (outline with its real openings, the AK's wood and magazine, the Glock's slide), drawn by `drawAKTraced` / `drawGlockTraced` with shading, grain, magazine ribs, the milled cut, pins, vents, serrations and grip texture (photos and drawn parts side by side looked wrong, so it's all drawn). What's fitted goes over them in real mm (`drawGunPic`, `drawAKAtt`, `drawGlockAtt`, `GUN_SPEC`: `x0`/`y0` the butt or slide rear and bore line, `k` px per mm; AK-47 880 mm / 415 mm barrel, 1050 mm with the can; Glock 17 202 mm, 186 mm slide, 114 mm barrel), and a row per attachment with a button: kills needed, BUY for medals, FIT or REMOVE. Tapping a row previews it on the drawing.

## Gear locker and night vision
- Gear locker (`GEAR`, `MAP.locker`, `lockerPiece`, `LOCKER`, `lockerStep`, `lockerAct`, `drawLocker`, `GEAR_ART`, `drawLockerDoors`): a steel locker on the armory's east wall with an olive GEAR LOCKER ring. It opens like the workbench (`openBench('locker')`: `bench.kind`, `benchStand(kind)` is where you walk to, you face east, its doors swing open, `lockerPiece.open`). Each item unlocks after `missions` completed (`prog.missions`: +1 in `travel` when you drive back from a cleared home), then costs medals. Putting something on is played out (`bench.la`, `pose.locker` in `drawChar`): the support hand reaches into the locker, brings it out (`drawHeld` 'nods' / 'nade:id') and mounts it on the helmet or stows it in a pouch. Progress keeps `gear` (owned) and `wear`.
  - `nvg`: L3Harris AN/PVS-14 monocular on a Wilcox L4 G24 mount, 6 missions + 30 medals. Worn (`prog.wear.nvg`, `lockShown`), it flips down on missions and up at home (`nvgStep`, `p.nvgK`, `NVG.flip`; N or the NVG button top right, `nvgBtn`, flips it up). Through it (`nvgOn`), being one tube, only a narrow field ahead of you is boosted (`NVG.half` either side of where you face, edges fading over `NVG.soft`): in it you make out people in the dark out to `NVG.see` (`pDarkR(off)`), the view reaches `NVG.view` at `NVG.k` (`beam.nvg`, carved in wedges in `drawLightMap`), and the picture is white phosphor (`drawNvgView`: fully desaturated, cool white, a raised black floor, crawling grain, in the same wedge) with the tube's edge as a dark rim along both sides of the cone (`band`, fading in toward the middle and out into the naked-eye dark). On screen everything outside the cone is black (your eye is behind the tube), except a small grey circle round you at its eyepiece. (For seeing people, outside the cone the naked-eye range still applies.) Drawn on the helmet by `drawNods` (one tube over the right eye when down, folded back when up).
  - `m84` (M84 stun grenade) and `mk3` (Mk3A2 offensive concussion grenade): bought once, then you carry `n` of each (`p.nades`, in `kitNow`), shown in pouches on the carrier's left side (`pose.nades`), topped up at the locker or the armory restock (`kitFull` counts them). See Grenades.

## Grenades
- M84 stun grenades and Mk3A2 offensive concussion grenades (`NADE`, `nades`, `p.nade`, `startNade`, `nadeStep`, `nadePress`/`nadeRelease`/`nadeCancel`, `nadeLaunch`, `nadeMove`, `updateNades`, `bangM84`, `blastMk3`): bought at the gear locker, carried in pouches (`p.nades`).
  - Picking one: hold reload; the picker now offers every option in an arc (`swapOpts`: the other gun and each grenade you carry, with its count). The Glock goes back in its holster first (`ph: 'wait'`); the rifle drops onto its sling and the right hand pulls the grenade from the pouch (`draw`, then `ready`).
  - Throwing: the reload button becomes THROW (G on a keyboard), press and hold (slide the same finger sideways to turn and aim the throw, `nadeX` into `turnAccum` like the aim stick, so one thumb aims and powers it): the left hand pulls the pin (`nade_pin`), the throwing arm cocks back as power builds over `NADE.wind` s (`pow`, shown as a ring on the button), and the arc it will fly is dotted on the floor with a ring at the landing (`nadePredict`, `drawNadeArc`, the same physics run ahead). Let go: it's thrown overhand (speed and lob from `vMin`..`vMax`, `vzMin`..`vzMax`), the spoon pings off (`nade_spoon`, its own little piece) and the fuze starts; then the rifle comes back up (`stow`). PUT AWAY (bottom left, by the move stick, `putBtn`; or R) stows it unthrown. The hands are drawn in `drawChar` from `pose.nade` (`nadePose`); the red cone hides and nothing fires while it's out.
  - In flight it has height and tumbles (`z`, `tb`), sub-stepped so it never tunnels: it bounces off walls, columns and crates (`NADE.rest`), breaks window glass it meets above the sill (or bounces off the wall under it), bounces off door slabs and shoves them (`shoveDoorAt`), off tall furniture and anything low it's too low to clear (`NADE.low`), and off people (`bodyZ`); on the floor it bounces (`floor`) then rolls to a stop (`roll`), with a clank each time (`nade_bounce`, the spoon `case_hard`), which bots can hear. Drawn by `drawNade` (the M84's holed olive body, the Mk3A2's black fibre body with metal caps, fuze, spoon and ring) with a shadow that drops away as it rises (`drawNades`).
  - M84 (`fuze` 1.5 s): a huge white flash (`light`) and a bang (`nade_flash`). Anyone with a line to it within `flash` is blinded by how close and how directly they were looking (through NVGs `NVG.blind` times longer); within `deaf` deafened (less through a wall). You: a whiteout with the frame at the flash burnt in (`grabBlindFrame`, `drawBlind`, `blindT`), no auto-fire while blind, and everything muffled (`sndDeafLP`, `deafT`) under a ringing (`sfxRing`, straight into the mix). Bots and the ally: `stun` (`b.stun`): reeling, blind, a hand over the eyes, then they investigate where it went off.
  - Mk3A2 (`fuze` 3.2 s): a blast (`nade_boom`, the distant take further off), a fireball flash, smoke and fibre fragments, a scorch burnt into the floor (`scorch`). Within `kill` it kills or downs whoever's there, plates or not (`blastKill`); out to `stun` it shoves, stuns, rattles and can crack a helmet; walls stop it, a shut door or window lets some through. Windows within `glass` blow out, doors within `door` are blown open, furniture is damaged and shoved. Bots that see a live one near them scramble away from it (`b.evade`).
  - Not yet: bots and the ally throwing grenades.

## Camera and balance
- Slow motion (`SLOWMO`, `slowMo`, `slowStep`, `timeK`, `gameT`): the hit that kills or downs the last enemy on the map (the moment `cleared` turns true) slows game time to `k` for `hold` real seconds, easing in and out. A downed last enemy triggers it on the downing shot, not when it later bleeds out. `frame` scales dt by it and runs its own game clock (`gameT`) for `update`/`render`; sounds started meanwhile play slower and deeper (`sndPlay` rate × √timeK).
- 60fps rAF loop: `update(dt, t)` then `render(t)`; dt is clamped to 0..0.05. `frame` queues the next frame first and catches errors (logs the first 5), so one bad frame can't freeze the game.
- Player sits at `ANCHOR_Y = 0.84` down the screen; the world rotates so the player's facing is always up.
- Scale `S = min(VH/345, VW/600)`; `STEADY_ZOOM = 0.86`. The view ahead (~290px moving, ~337 braced) reaches past anyone's firing range.
- Look-ahead (`LOOK`, `look`): while the aim pad is held (or Q/E), the camera drifts a little with the drag: forward while held, a bit more when dragging up, sideways with the drag. In `render` it's clamped so you stay `LOOK.margin` inside the screen.
- Inspect (`INSPECT`, `inspect`): when calm (still/braced, not aiming, no enemy visible, not suppressed, no shot for 1 s) for `INSPECT.delay` s, the camera eases in (`INSPECT.zoom`) and centres you (`INSPECT.anchor`) so you can read your plates and helmet. Anything happening pulls it straight back out.
- **Sight and reach roughly match what's on screen, for everyone**: `CONE_R = 300` (screen shows ≈290px straight ahead, more to the sides and with look-ahead/brace), `STEADY_RANGE = 0.15`. `RED_R = 280` (lock range) and `BOT.fireRange = RED_R`; bots see with `CONE_R` too. The squad distances (`flankR`, `coverDist`, `wideDist`, `findShadow`) are sized to these. If the camera changes, re-match them.

## Run flow and maps
- Maps (`MAPS`, `MAP`, `applyMap`, `travel(name)`): every location is its own map of the same 36×30 size, with:
  - `solid`/`floor` grids, `spawn` (tiles), `doors` (`[x0, y0, x1, y1, selfClosing]`)
  - `fixtures` (light fixture rows in px), `loot` (spots by kind), `bots` (spawn tiles)
  - `exit`: `veh` (the vehicle it belongs to, a piece's `veh`) and `door` (how far along its body), or fixed tiles `x`/`y` as a fallback; `r`, `hold`, `to`, `needClear`, `label`, banner `title`/`sub`
- `travel` swaps `MAP` and runs `resetGame(kit)`: the world is rebuilt but your kit comes with you (`kitNow`: plates with their damage, helmet, rifle mag, vest mags). Leaving for a mission saves `deployKit`, and a restart after dying there uses it.
- Armory restock (`MAP.restock`, `restockStep`, `RESTOCK`, `kitFull`): stand in the blue RESTOCK ring at the house's gear crates and fresh kit goes on one piece at a time through the normal equip animations. Order: helmet, plates (weaker side first), vest mags, then a reload. Replaced gear goes back on the rack (`equip.rack`: nothing dropped). The ring reads STOCKED when you're full. Map-specific furniture and floor decals are switched on `MAP.id` in `buildProps` and `buildFloorArt`.
  - `hotel`: parked (not reachable: not in `MISSIONS`; its data and code stay for later). Once it's clear (`cleared`), the "AREA CLEAR / Get in the taxi" banner shows and the exit ring opens at the taxi's rear door (`veh: 'taxi'`); it leads to the house.
  - `home` (generated, see Generated missions): a raid on a modern home. Cleared, its exit is your car's driver door and leads back to the house.
  - `house`: the safe house. Bedroom (double bed, bedside tables and lamp, wardrobe, bookshelf, reading chair), bathroom (vanity, bath, toilet), armory (crates, the workbench (see Attachments and the workbench), weapon rack, and the back door to the backyard), living room (TV that glows, sofa, armchair, bookshelf, plants), kitchen (counter on the south wall so the armory door opens onto clear floor, fridge, table with chairs), backyard (grill, bench, plants, a gate in the back fence), front yard and street with your car. No enemies and no headcount in the HUD. Deploying is getting into your car: the exit ring is at its driver door out front (`veh: 'mine'`) and leads to the next mission (`to: 'mission'`, see Generated missions). Its own fixtures: porch light, floor lamp, kitchen hood, bathroom mirror, armory tube, desk lamp, EXIT sign. Built to grow features later.
- Credits (`CREDITS`, `creditsBtn`, `creditLines`, `drawCredits`, `credits`): an "i" button left of the speaker opens a full-screen list of every asset source (CC-BY with full attribution first, then CC0 sounds, then public-domain images/references). The game pauses while it's open (`frame` skips `update` and the clock); drag, wheel or arrow keys scroll, X or Esc closes. Add every new asset to `CREDITS`.
- At the safe house there's no edge arrow to your car (`drawExtractHud` skips it there).
- Exits belong to vehicles (`mkExit`): the ring sits just outside the vehicle's door on the kerb side and follows the vehicle (`EXIT.car`), ready for drivable vehicles later. The exit ring (`EXIT`, `exitOpen`, `drawExtract`) and its edge arrow with distance (`drawExtractHud`) show while it's open. Standing in it for `hold` s travels (`extractT`). The banner shows for `BANNER_T` s.
- States: `play`, `downed` (bleeding out, see Downed), `dead`, `dry` (out of ammo; unreachable while the Glock has unlimited mags). `dead`/`dry` show an overlay, and tap/Enter restarts the current map.

## Generated missions
- The game starts at the safe house. `MISSIONS = ['home']` (the hotel is parked): `travel('mission')` builds a brand new home (`MAPS.home = homeMap()`), and a restart after dying in a home (`restart`) builds another one, so you never get the same house twice (`usedSeeds`, random seeds up to 2^31). The banner just says "Get in your car to deploy", nothing about what's next.
- `genHome(seed)` (seeded `rng32`) builds a single-storey home on the 36×30 grid, as map data shaped like `MAPS.hotel` (plus `gen`, `seed`, `size`, `plan`, `rooms`, `hostages`, `civs`, `props`, `decals`, `top`, `clutter`). Every seed is a different floor plan, built like real houses:
  - Size (`HOMEGEN.sizes`, rolled per seed): small (17–21 × 12–15 tiles, 1–3 enemies, 2–3 residents, 1–2 bedrooms), medium (20–25 × 14–17, 2–4, 2–5, 2–3 bedrooms), large (24–29 × 16–19, 3–5, 3–6, 3–4 bedrooms): a few gunmen holding a household, usually more residents than enemies; each also rolls an en-suite, office, laundry, dining room and attached garage by its own chances.
  - Plan (`HOMEGEN.plans`): `center` (a hallway from the front door to the back, public rooms one side, bedrooms the other), `cross` (a hallway across the house, living room and a bedroom at the front, kitchen, bath and bedrooms behind), `side` (open-plan kitchen and living on one side, a bedroom hallway along it opening onto the living room), `wing` (the same but the bedroom block is set back: an L). Any but `wing` can get an attached garage (`+garage`: front-aligned, a door into the house, an open garage door and a drive).
  - Rooms come from real floor areas (`ROOMS`, m², a tile = 0.81 m², with in-game minimums) shared out along each side of the hallway by `strip`; `stripFit` drops optional rooms (storage, laundry, office, en-suite, dining, extra bedroom) when a side is too short and adds real ones (bedroom, office, storage, bath; dining or storage on the public side) when it's too long, so rooms don't just stretch. The hallway sits where both sides get their share of area. Doors off the hallway, wide openings for living/dining (and often the kitchen), the en-suite through its bedroom. Front door at the hallway's front end or into the living room, back door from the kitchen or hallway. Windows on every plain stretch of outside wall (not hallways or the garage).
  - Furniture at real sizes (`REAL`, cm → px at 0.9 m a tile) stood against walls by type (`against`, `anyWall`, `bestWalls`: walls without doors, and without windows for beds and TVs, come first). Pieces carry `face` (the wall their back is against; `drawFurniture` turns the upright drawing to suit; the toilet uses `rot`). Home pieces with a `face` get real-proportion drawings from `drawHomePiece` (bed with one or two pillows, duvet over the sides and a throw; sofa with arms, back and seat cushions and throw pillows; armchair; media console with a flat-screen, soundbar and box; desk with monitor, keyboard, mouse and lamp; bathtub with taps at one end; vanity with an oval basin). Home dining tables are bare wood with placemats.
  - `homeMap(seed)` rerolls until `genHome` passes its own check (a tile flood fill from the street, heavy furniture blocking, must reach every door, bot, civilian, room and the car's ring); ~45% pass first time, about 1 ms each.
- Floor codes added for homes: `l` light oak (living), `b` bedroom carpet, `n` bath tile, `v` lawn (outdoor), `w` hallway. `OUTDOOR` is `'saRv'`.
- Residents are hostages (`MAP.hostages`, `c.hostage`): they start cowering, placed at least 4 tiles from the gunmen when there's room, don't panic at gunmen who were already there, and only run when shots go off close by (`CIV.near`). Bots only shoot a hostage who's running (`botShootsCiv`) and start hunting them later than in the hotel (`HUNT` delays ×2 first, ×1.5 after).
- Patrols (`pickWaypoint`) mostly stay indoors on every map (outdoor waypoints skipped 90% of the time).

## Map
- 36×30 tiles, `T = 48` px (~0.9 m per tile), rooms at real-world sizes. Two layers: `MAP_SOLID` and `MAP_FLOOR` (char grids).
- Solid chars: `.` open, `#` wall (destructible, border is not), `E` elevator, `C` column, `X` crate, `G`/`g` window / broken window, `S` sofa, `D` desk, `B` bar, `K` counter, `t` table, `P` planter, `L` luggage cart. (Cars and the taxi used to be `c`/`y` tiles; they're objects now, see Furniture and props.)
- Floor codes: `m` lobby marble, `M` mat, `e` elevator hall, `w` restaurant oak, `k` kitchen tile, `u` restroom tile, `o` office carpet, `r` lounge carpet, `z` back-of-house concrete, `s` sidewalk, `R` red carpet, `a` asphalt. Colours in `FLOOR_COL`.
- Layout (north to south):
  - Back corridor (y 1–2) with crates.
  - North band (y 4–9): kitchen (x 1–7), men's (9–11), elevator hall (13–18, elevators and stair door in the north wall), women's (20–22), office (24–27), meeting room (29–34).
  - South band (y 11–20): restaurant (1–8), lobby (10–20, reception desk, column, sofa corner, medallion), lounge bar (22–34, bar/back bar, piano, tables, sofa).
  - Facade with windows (y 21), sidewalk and red carpet (y 22–24), street with parked cars and a taxi (y 25–28).
  - Arches link the lobby to the restaurant, lounge and elevator hall.
- Hotel: spawn on the red carpet at tile (15.5, 23.5) facing north. 5 bots (`makeBots` from `MAP.bots`).
- Map-specific data to keep in sync when a layout changes: the map's entry in `MAPS` (spawn, doors, bots, exit, fixtures: the hotel's are `HOTEL_FIXTURES`, loot: none on either map; pickups only come from enemies), its floor decals in `buildFloorArt`, and its hard-coded props in `buildProps`.
- All furniture is objects (`pieces`), not tiles: `buildPieces` turns every furniture run in the map grid into one and clears its tiles (columns `C`, crates `X`, elevators `E` and windows `G` stay structural tiles).
  - Built-ins (runs bigger than 4 tiles, and every `B` bar or `K` counter): `builtIn` makes a bolted-down object (`fixed`, `builtin`, never wrecks) of real depth (`BUILT_DEPTH`): a 1-tile-deep run sits against the wall it backs onto (or centred if free), ends that meet more of the same (the bar's L, the back bar) reach in to join it, bigger blocks are inset. Its `kind` keeps the drawing: `meeting` table (`D` 4+ wide), `reception` desk (`D` 5 tall), `backbar`. They're drawn live from their sprite like any piece and work as cover, block bullets by chance, catch casings and blood, and block bot paths (`rebuildOcc`), all with their true shape. The map grid only places them.
  - Smaller runs are movable pieces, nudged a little off the grid (up to their inset) so they don't sit dead on the tiles.
- `buildFloorArt()` paints floors, details, the remaining structural built-ins (windows, columns, crates, elevators) and wall tops once into an offscreen canvas (`FLOOR_ART` / `artG`) at `ART_RES` (2) pixels per world px. `artG` carries that scale, so everything draws in world px, and the render draws it at `W*T × H*T`.
- Wall tops (`WALL_TOP` by `wallMat`, texture, a lit bevel and a dark outline where they meet a room) are shown over the darkness, clipped to `WALL_PATH` and dimmed by `WALL_SHADE`, so walls (and the bullet marks on them) read as structure instead of black voids. Every permanent mark (debris, bullet marks, blood, wrecks) is stamped into it later.
- `drawFurniture(g, c, x, y, w, h, n, f, room)` draws every furniture/prop type, with detail chosen by `room` and the piece's `kind`.
- Materials (`FINISH`, `makeTex`, `TEX`, `finishCanvas`, `finishedImage`, `pieceSprite`):
  - Grey semi-transparent 64px textures (wood, soft, carpet, metal, thin, marble, ceramic, stone, concrete, asphalt) are laid over flat colours.
  - Every furniture image is textured by material (`FURN[c].mat` or `BUILTIN_MAT`), lit from one side, colour-muted and given a baked soft drop shadow.
  - Built-ins are finished into the floor art. Movable pieces are rendered once into a cached image (`f.spr`, at `FINISH.res` = 3×, made for every piece in `initWorld` so nothing is built mid-fight) and blitted each frame (bites still clip it).
  - Floors get `FLOOR_TEX` per floor code plus faint per-tile tone variation, and soft ambient-occlusion shadows along wall bases.
  - The map starts clean: no litter, stains or cracks are baked in. Dirt comes only from play (casings, debris, bullet marks, blood, bloody boot prints, wrecks, furniture scrapes).

## Furniture and props

## Doors
Doorways with physics hinge leaves, pushed by bodies with torque. Each leaf's swing is capped each way (`leafLimit`, `l.hi`/`l.lo`, at most `DOOR.max`) where it would start to enter a wall, window, column or crate (the leaf from 8 px out, ±1 px; about 82° in a plain wall), so an open door never sits inside the wall beside it. Restroom, kitchen, fire-exit and street doors (and a home's front and back doors) self-close, but the closer waits while anyone is within the leaf's swing (`inSwing`), so it never swings back onto you in the doorway; office and meeting-room doors stay where left. Bullets can swing or destroy them. Creak noise when a latch opens. A leaf standing open across a hallway, pushed against its stop by someone walking along the hallway (`alongWall`: moving along the door's wall line, not through the doorway), is pulled shut out of their way for `DOOR.pullT` s (`l.pull`, `DOOR.pull`), so anyone can get through a door that opens toward them. `DOOR.oneWay` (real inward-opening doors) is off: bots got trapped behind them.

## Lighting (render order matters)
1. Scene pass: floor art (only the on-screen part), pieces, doors, items, debris, pools, ragdolls, visible bots, gun smoke. Everything is culled to `VIEW` (`onView`), the world rect on screen this frame.
2. Light map: a canvas at `LIGHT.res` (0.5) of screen resolution, scaled up when laid over, filled with darkness (`LIGHT.ambient = 0.82`), carved out with `destination-out` by every light's wall-occluded polygon (`lightPoly`, `carve`), then laid over the scene. Light and view polygons (`visPoly`, used by `lightPoly` and `rayPoly`) cast an even fan plus a ray pair either side of every wall corner (`lightCorners`, rebuilt when walls change) and door edge, so shadow edges sit exactly on corners and don't shimmer as you move.
3. Glow/info pass: coloured additive tints (`glowLights`), wall edges caught by the beam, red aiming cone, sound rings, bot labels, tracers and sparks, then the player (always drawn after the darkness).
4. Suppression post-effect, then the HUD.
- Weapon lights are an attachment (`att.light` on `p` and each bot, `hasLight`); only the Glock's X300U (see Attachments) is one so far, so usually the only cone on screen is the red firing cone. Without a light you see an evenly dim, wall-blocked area all round (`beam.eyes`, `SEE.darkView`/`darkK`, flat out to `SEE.darkFlat` of the radius, then fading): no halo around you, and `lightAt` darkens you to the same level as the floor you stand on. A person in front of your eyes (`SEE.fov` either side of where you face, the same eyes bots have; not just the narrow rifle cone) is made out in the dark only within `SEE.dark` px (just inside the dim view, `SEE.darkView`); anyone lit by a lamp, sign or muzzle flash is seen at full range. Nobody gets lit up or noticed by their own light. Everything below about beams applies only to characters with a light. Lasers etc. are planned as further attachments.
- With a light: everyone's weapon light is on. Bot beams (`botBeam`, carved in `drawLightMap`) only show where you have line of sight (clipped to `beam.view`). A bot's light pointed at you shows a glare at its lamp, even when the bot is dark (`glowLights`).
- Seeing is light-based for everyone (`SEE`, `litByScene`, `inBeam`): you're seen if you're in the viewer's beam, within the close bubble, lit by any other light (`frameLights` = lamps/signs/flashes from the last render, plus everyone else's beam), or shining your light at the viewer. Bots also need you inside their eyes (`SEE.fov`) unless already in a fight (`botSees`). The player sees bots by the same rules (`isLit` and the bot-visibility loop). A bot just seen stays visible for 0.35 s while the line is clear (`b.seenT`), so nothing pops in and out at the edge of sight or under a flickering light. Drawing is separate from detection: any bot you have a clear line to (within `viewR * 1.6`) is drawn (`b.vis`), under the darkness layer, so it simply fades with how lit it is and is never cut out. Only walls hide it. Lock-on, labels (`b.know`), threat markers and the inspect calm check use real detection (`b.seen`).
- Suspicion (`noticeBeam`, `beamSpots`): a patrolling bot that sees your beam land in front of it but not you goes to search halfway between the lit spot and you, with a cooldown of `SEE.noticeCd` seconds.
- Doors and light: `lightCast` ignores doorway tiles (`lightBlock`) and stops at the actual door slabs (`doorSegs`, refreshed each render), so swung doors cast shadows and shut doors catch the beam on their near face. Sight checks (`isLit`, `botSees`, lock-on) use the same cast. `opq` still marks shut doorways for sound.
- Ceiling lights (`ceil` fixtures: white trim, frosted dome): every room and hallway in a generated home has one (two along a room 8+ tiles long, one per ~5 tiles of hallway; the garage a `tube`), sized to the room. It's night, so each is on or switched off by room type (`CEIL.on`: living room 80%, kitchen 75%, hallway 60%, bedrooms 30-35%, storage 15%...). A switched-off light (`unlit`, 13th fixture field) shows grey glass, gives no light and can still be shot out (no sputter). The safe house has one in every room, all on. A fixed light's polygon is cached (`F.poly`, `F.polyKey`) until a door near it moves or the walls change (`edgeVer`).
- Light sources: muzzle flashes (`flashes[]`, ~0.06s, also reveal the shooter; like every light, only those that can reach the screen get a polygon), and only real, visible, breakable fixtures, no free-floating lights. `FIXTURES`: EXIT signs over doors, the back-bar LED strip, elevator indicators, the flickering corridor tube, reception desk lamp, kitchen hood lamps, restroom mirror light bars. Each has a housing (`drawFixtures(false)`) and a lit face drawn over the darkness (`drawFixtures(true)`), plus hp. Rounds passing through take hp and carry on (`hitFixtures` in `stepBullet`). At 0 it sputters out (`die`), throwing sparks and glass. `resetFixtures` on restart. Furniture lights: street lamps, floor lamps, vending machine (`f.light`, gone when the piece breaks) and desk monitors. Weapon lights only with `att.light`.

- Downed (`DOWN`, `goDown`, `downStep`, `endDown`, `smear`, `hitDowned`): a lethal hit downs the target alive with `DOWN.chance` instead of killing outright. The ragdoll falls as usual, then stays alive (`rg.alive`) for `DOWN.life` s, weakening as it goes:
  - Motion is kinematic and eased, never random kicks: joints glide toward targets in the body's own frame (`bodyFrame`, `bodyPt`, `glide`), the body is heavily damped while alive (`RAG.fricAlive`), it breathes, and there are long still moments.
  - Lying there it keeps moving, one or two gestures at a time (`DOWN_GEST`, `DOWN.two`), with short pauses (`gestGap`): a hand clamping the wound, a knee drawn up then the leg sliding straight, curling up both knees, writhing (shoulders rolling to one side and back), lifting the head, an arm reaching out and flopping back. Deep breathing (`DOWN.breath`). Gestures get slower and rarer as it weakens.

Once there, it stays and moves as if lying there. You steer yourself with the move stick.

  - It moves in strokes (plant something, haul the whole body past it, rest; rests lengthen as it weakens), in one of four styles (`DOWN.modes`; you always use `crawl`):
    - `crawl`: hand over hand, the same-side knee pushing.
    - `oneArm`: always the same arm, the other hand clamped on the wound, the opposite leg pushing; slower.
    - `frog`: both hands reach while both knees splay up, then one big pull and kick.
    - `heels`: on its back, heels and elbows dug in, pushing itself along head first, twisting its shoulders to steer; if a wall stops it, it rolls over and crawls.
  - A stroke scraping a wall angles up to ±0.5 rad off it. It stops cleanly (`C.done = 1`) if every angle hits a wall, or once the pull would stretch a planted arm or leg past its length, so no limb is ever dragged out of joint. It turns along a wall it can't pass, or gives up. It stops crawling when too weak (`DOWN.crawlTo`). A crawl that gets no closer to its next waypoint for `DOWN.stuckT` s skips it. The trail is a blood smear painted into the floor art (`trailStep`).
  - A body that went down alive keeps live joint ranges (`RAG_LIMITS_ALIVE`: knees fold, legs draw up, no hip-to-foot spacer) through death, so it dies as it lay.
  - More gestures: rolling onto one side and back, both hands pulling at the vest, and one last reach near the end.
  - It then bleeds out and settles like any death.
  - A downed bot counts as dead for everyone (`b.dead`): no lock-on, no bot targets it, the headcount drops and the area can clear. Its loot drops only when it bleeds out. A stray round that hits a downed body finishes it.
  - You can go down too: state `downed`. Bots stop seeing you (`botSees`), intel and roles are cleared, and the world keeps running (`live`). The move stick drags you, the camera follows your body, there's no cone, and a "BLEEDING OUT" vignette closes in until state turns `dead`.
  - All ragdolls, yours included, are drawn under the darkness.

Rounds hit civilians like anyone else (`takeHit`, ragdoll in their own clothes via `civRagDetail`/`civRagEnd`, and they can go down and crawl). Your auto-fire won't shoot through one: they block the cone like a teammate (`friendInLine`, the cone turns blue). They don't count toward the headcount.

## Rescue
- Once the hotel is clear (`cleared`), walking up to a surviving civilian secures them on its own (`RESCUE`, `rescueSecure`, `p.secure`): they kneel facing away (`restrain`, `c.restrainF`), your support hand goes to their wrists (`doorHand` in the pose), and after `RESCUE.T` s they're zip-tied (`c.bound`, hands behind the back with a white tie). Then they `follow` you (`walkTo` along a path). Moving off cancels it, and they cower again. HUD: "CIVILIANS SECURED x / y" (`drawRescueHud`).
- Extracting takes every secured civilian with you (`carried`, look keys, set in `travel`). At the safe house they appear with you (`houseCivs`), and a US Army officer (`OFFICER_LOOK`: OCP, patrol cap, subdued captain's bars, parade rest) waits out back, in the backyard behind the house (`RESCUE.officer`), with an OFFICER tag under him like the ally's, reached through the armory's back door. HUD: "BRING n CIVILIANS TO THE OFFICER OUT BACK (THROUGH THE ARMORY)".
- Coming within `RESCUE.handR` of him starts the handover (`startHandover`): they gather round him, then all leave through the back gate (`RESCUE.away`) and are gone. A "CIVILIANS HANDED OVER" banner shows with the medals earned (`MEDALS.perCiv` each, saved, spent at the workbench), and `handedOver` keeps the running total (not saved between sessions). Every time they leave, the officer says "Do better." (`c.say`, `RESCUE.sayT`), shown as text over him in the visual-sound mode (muted) until there's a recording. Anyone not handed over is still with you next time you come home.

## Melee
- Automatic knife, for you and the bots (`MELEE`, `c.melee` per character, `startMelee`, `stepMelee`, `meleeTarget`, `meleeKill`, `endMelee`, `knifePose`): an enemy within `MELEE.range` in front (inside `MELEE.cone`, nothing in between) gets the knife on its own, like the auto-fire. You go for bots instantly; a bot goes for you only after you've been in reach and in front of it for `MELEE.react` s, and only while you're up (`state === 'play'`). No firing during it.
- Phases (`pose.knife`, drawn in `drawChar`, knife by `drawKnife`):
  - `draw`: the rifle swings aside on its sling and the support hand pulls the knife from the chest.
  - `lunge`: a step in and a thrust to the chest.
  - `hold` (`MELEE.hold` = 1.4 s): the target dies (`killBot`, never downed). Its ragdoll is pinned at the chest to the blade (`rg.pin`, enforced in `ragStep`), so the body hangs on the knife, limbs going limp, sagging out in front and to the side, dripping blood.
  - While the body is on the blade it's a shield: rounds stop in it (`hitDowned` also checks pinned bodies → `shield`: blood and a jolt). Its weight slows the killer: you move at `MELEE.carry` and turn at `MELEE.carryTurn` of normal; bots stand still while knifing.
  - `pull`: the knife is wrenched free with a gout of blood, and the body drops away.
  - `stow`: the knife is sheathed and the hand goes back to the rifle.
- A target that gets out of reach before the thrust is missed. Killing the attacker mid-attack cancels it and drops whoever was on its blade (`endMelee` in `killBot` and on your death). A pin never lasts more than `MELEE.pinMax` s.
- A bot's knife kills you outright (plates don't stop it): you die pinned on its blade (`state = 'dead'`). The bot still finishes pull and stow after you're dead. Its draw and lunge give your auto-fire about 0.4 s to drop it first.

## Protection (there is no health bar)
- Everyone starts with `newProt()`: front plate, back plate (`PLATE_HP = 52`, ~3 rifle hits each) and a helmet (`HELMET_HP = 35`, ~2 hits).
- `takeHit(c, dmg, hx, hy, ux, uy)`: a round from the front half hits the front plate, from behind the back plate. A plate chips (`chipsF`/`chipsB`), throws ceramic fragments and finally shatters, leaving an empty bag. With that side's plate gone, the helmet takes hits, cracks, and is knocked off as floor debris. The next unprotected hit kills.
- Loot items: `mag` (`rounds`), `plate` and `helmet` (`hp`, `chips`). The dead drop their rifle mag, vest mags, any plate still above 0 and their helmet if it's still on. You only pick up upgrades (`canTake`), the biggest upgrade in reach first (`pickGain`): a plate goes into your weaker side, and whatever is swapped out is dropped on the floor.
- Nobody stoops for loot in a fight (`PICKUP`, `fighting(c)`, `c.fightAt`: within `PICKUP.calm` s of shooting, being hit, suppressed (`supp`/your `tun`) or an enemy in sight; the ally while in `fight` mode): no new grabs, and a grab or gear swap already under way is dropped (`cancelPickup`) so the hands go back on the gun. Only with the rifle and vest both empty may they grab, and then only mags. Applies to you, bots (mag scooping in `botArms`, plate/helmet grabs in `botGear`/sweep) and the ally.
- Picking up (`updatePickup`, `PICK_TIME`, `EQUIP`, `equip`, `stepEquip`): walk over loot and you scoop it up on the move. Once the grab starts it's committed, and the hand carries the item while it's stowed or put on, wherever you walk. A mag is stowed in a chest pouch. A helmet or plate is swapped: the new one is tucked against the body, the old one comes off and is dropped at `EQUIP[k].off` (you're unprotected there until `on`), the new one goes in, then the strap/flap is done. While gear goes on (`gearBusy`) the rifle hangs to the side: no shooting, no reloading. No grabbing anything mid-reload. Reload button / R (`reloadPressed`) cancels what the hands are doing so you can shoot (`cancelPickup`: gear already in place stays, anything in hand drops at your feet, and nearby loot is skipped (`it.skip`) until you step away; a reload is cancelled only before the old mag is out). With the rifle empty it cancels and reloads; with no ammo at all it doesn't cancel. The button shows an X while a tap would cancel. Shared by everyone: `gearStep`, `newEquip`, `gearPose`.
- Bots do the same for plates and helmets (`b.grab`, `b.equip`, `botGear`): they stand still with the rifle down and can't fire or reload. A grab is dropped if the item vanishes or you show up, but once the gear is going on they finish it. A bot killed mid-swap drops the piece it was putting on. Mags still go straight into a pouch (`botTake`).
- Hand animation (`drawChar`): the support hand follows eased `[time, point]` keys; `drawHeld` draws what's in it (mag, helmet, plate, an attachment). It rests on the vertical grip when one is fitted (`foreAt`), else the handguard, and rides back with the recoil. Reload: cant the rifle, strip the old mag and toss it, dig the next from the chest pouch, seat it (the rifle bumps as it seats), rack the charging handle (a jolt as it's let go). Standing still the rifle sways slowly with your breathing (`pose.breath`, less right after firing).

## Magazines
- Everyone carries 4 mags: 1 in the rifle (`ammo` / `b.ammo`) and `MAG_VEST = 3` in the vest (`mags` / `b.mags`, rounds per mag). There's no loose ammo pool.
- Reloading takes the fullest vest mag (`pullMag`). The old mag is dropped (`ejectMag`): empty ones become clutter, one with rounds left becomes a `mag` item anyone can pick up.
- With a full vest you can still swap in a fuller mag, dropping your emptiest. Bots scoop up the fullest mag in reach as they walk, but not the one they just dropped. There's no loot lying around the maps: every pickup comes off a fallen enemy.

## Sidearm
- Glock 17 (`PISTOL`: 17-round mag, unlimited spares, 9mm `dmg`, `rate`, `spread`, `speed`, `reload`, `draw`; `c.side` per character, `freshSide`, `sideTick` (draw/holster/reload, shared), `sideStep` + `pistolShoot` (you), `startPistolReload` (R)). Everyone carries one.
- It comes out when you pick it (`p.side.want = 'pistol'`, see Weapon swap: that cancels a rifle reload or gear going on) or on its own when the rifle is dry (rifle and vest empty): the rifle swings aside on its sling, the hand drops to the holster, and the Glock comes up two-handed (`p.side.sw`, `out` flips halfway through `PISTOL.draw`). When you pick the rifle again (or, if the Glock came out because the rifle was dry, the moment the rifle has a mag again) it goes back in the holster and the rifle comes up and carries on (an empty rifle reloads itself). Auto-fires on lock like the rifle, reloads itself when empty (the empty mag drops out, the support hand fetches a fresh 17 from the pouch; there's always another), R reloads it early. The empty drops to the floor like an AK mag (`dropMag(..., 'pmag')`, a straight Glock mag that gets painted in). 9 mm is weaker against armour and cover: rounds carry `cal: 'pistol'` (`makeBullet`), plates and helmets take only `PISTOL.armor` (0.3) of the damage (`takeHit(..., armK)`: ~14 hits to break a plate vs 3 from the AK), and `passThrough` lets them through cover `PISTOL.pen` (0.45) as often, losing more speed. An unarmoured hit still kills. The barrel is shorter (`gunReach(..., PISTOL.len, PISTOL.lat)`), casings come out of its own port.
- Its loaded rounds travel with your kit (`kitNow.side`) and the armory restock tops it up. With unlimited mags nobody runs dry: the `dry` state and `dryBot` are unreachable for now (kept for later). HUD shows its rounds and "GLOCK 17 · ∞ MAGS" while it's out.
- Bots too: `sideTick` runs in `updateBot`; while it's out `botShoot` fires single aimed shots (`PISTOL.rate`, its own damage, speed and casing port), rifle reloads and the knife wait, and `botReady` (the gun in hand loaded and up) gates every bot shot. `ammoLeft` counts the rounds in the Glock. Scooping a rifle mag holsters it again. Glock mags don't drop as loot.
- Holster always on the right hip, whichever shoulder the rifle is on (`drawHolster(L, 1, ...)`); the Glock is right-handed: the right arm draws and holds it and the left supports (`supS`/`supSg` switch to the left side while it's out), and the slung rifle goes to its own shoulder's side. Ragdolls keep it on the hip that was on their right (`rg.holJ`). It (`drawHolster`: kydex shell, straps, grip showing when it's in) swings on a spring (`HOL`, `p.hol`, `holsterStep`) driven by body acceleration, every footstep, recoil and busy hands (reloads, gear, knife, securing someone). `drawGlock` draws the pistol from above (slide serrations, port, sights, its own muzzle flash). Arms fold in near the body (`fold`) instead of flinging the elbows out.

## Movement, aim and cover
- Weight (`LOAD`, `loadOf`, `loadMul`, in real kg): plates 2.5 kg each and the helmet 1.5 while worn, every magazine by its rounds (`magKg`), each attachment (`LOAD.att`, also shown on the workbench rows), and your NVG and grenades. Speed, acceleration and stopping scale by `LOAD.per` per kg against `LOAD.ref` (both plates, helmet, four full mags, stock guns = base speed); more is slower, less is quicker. Bots follow the same rule (in `followPath`), their mags and kit included. The locker shows what you carry and your speed.
- Loadout (`prog.loadout`, `lo`, `LOADOUT0`, `vestCap`, `applyLoadout`, `setLoadout`, `kitChange`, `lockerFlush`): picked on the gear locker's LOADOUT row: plates both/front/none (`bareF`/`bareB`: an empty, flat bag), helmet on/off, 0–3 spare mags. Every change is played out piece by piece at the locker (`bench.la`, queued in `bench.lq`; the rifle hangs aside like any gear change): the support hand unclips the helmet and lifts it off (or brings one out and puts it on), slides a plate out of its bag (the back one over the shoulder) or in, pulls a mag from the chest pouch or stows one, carrying each to or from the locker, with the buckle, velcro and cloth sounds; the kit itself changes the moment it's on or off (`kitChange`), and leaving mid-way finishes the rest at once (`lockerFlush`). The restock, `kitFull`, pickups (`canTake`/`pickGain`/`stowMag`) and the vest HUD all keep to it, so you never pick up what you chose to leave behind.
- Momentum movement: `MOVE = { accel, decel, fwd: 1.0, side: 0.72, back: 0.5 }`, at a deliberate tactical pace (`p.speed` 140 px/s ≈ 2.6 m/s, bots patrol 70 / search 120).
- Stride: legs swing `STEP_LEN / (2 * CHAR_SCALE) * walk` and the phase advances per `STEP_LEN * walk` of travel, so planted boots don't skate at any speed. Moving while crouched/ducked, the legs come up out of the kneel into a duck-walk (`crL` in `drawChar`).
- Off-screen threats (`threats`, `drawThreats`): a shot from a bot you can't see puts a red marker at the screen edge pointing at it for `THREAT_T` s, but only in the visual-sound mode (`snd: true`, see Sound); the ally's CONTACT callouts always show.
- The support hand only reaches for doors, cover and loot in front of the body (`reachable` needs local x > 1; grab targets are clamped forward).
- Arms are a spring (`ARMS`, `armsStep`); `sway` is the rifle's offset from aim, `redAng = p.aim + sway`.
- The rifle is a physical object (`GUN`, `gunReach`, `barrelReach`, `c.gun`): a barrel `GUN.len` long from the shoulder line, stopped by walls, columns, crates, door slabs and glass still in a window pane, tall furniture (`TALL`: wardrobes, shelves, fridges, cabinets, vending machines, gun racks, stalls, showers; low pieces like tables, sofas, counters and cars pass under it) and the bodies of your own side and of bystanders (`BARREL_BODY`; an enemy in front of the muzzle doesn't push it aside, the downed and the dead lie below it) (`paneReach`: the window tile itself doesn't stop it, so at a window it reaches into the opening up to the pane, and through it once the glass there is shot out). Up close it's pulled in (`gun.in`, up to `maxIn`), then swung clear toward the open side (`gun.sw`/`sd`). Its tip shoves doors (`pushDoors(e, who)`). `gun.ang`/`mx`/`my` is the real barrel: rounds, the red cone (`rayPoly` from the muzzle), the lock check, smoke and flash all start at the muzzle along it. The barrel's base is the drawn shoulder: `c.x/y` (which already include a peek, so it's never added again), plus the crouch hunch forward and the wall lean sideways; the drawn rifle undoes the crouch's bladed turn (`CROUCH.blade` in `rel`). The drawn tip and `gun.mx/my` agree to within 0.5 px in every pose. Swung past `noFire` it can't shoot (`gunCanFire`). Friendly fire is on: rounds hit anyone they reach (bots hit bots; `allies` = your squad, see Ally). Nobody shoots through a teammate or a bystander (`friendInLine`, `FF`: the line from the muzzle widened by the scatter cone, counting only someone the rounds could actually reach: not past where the bore meets a wall, and with a clear line from the muzzle, so people behind walls or shut doors don't block): bots hold fire and side-step to open the line (`clearLine`); for you, auto-fire stops and the cone turns blue (`p.blocked`). Recoil: the round's deviation from the bore kicks the barrel the other way (`recoilKick(..., dev)`). Recoil slides the rifle back and kicks the camera (`RECOIL`, `springStep`).
- Cover (`COVER`, `senseCover`): wall hugging, mounting the rifle on corners and sills (stiffer, less recoil), switching shoulders away from cover (can't fire mid-switch), ducking behind objects. Your own shots clear the cover you're touching; cover is hit by shots at someone sheltering behind it, but soft cover can let them through (see Penetration).
- Crouch look (`CROUCH`): never drawn smaller; the pose shows it. Kneels on the rifle-side knee (that shin trailing behind, boot sole-up), front knee splayed out past the arm with a knee pad, upper body hunched forward and bladed toward the rifle side, shoulders rounded, head low over the knees, with a tighter, darker shadow. Going down and coming up runs on a spring (`cv.duck`/`cv.dv`, `CROUCH.k`/`c`): a small dip past full, then it settles.
- Peeking (`PEEK`, in `senseCover`): standing still with the view blocked within `PEEK.block` but open from a point `PEEK.dist` (11px) to one side → the character really shifts out that way (`cov.px/py` = body offset from the planted boots; eyes, muzzle and hitbox move with it). Drawn with boots planted, legs angled out, the dipped shoulder shaded, the head leading and the rifle canted. Bots peek by the same rule.

## Bullets, destruction, sound
- `makeBullet` / `stepBullet`: velocity with drag, ricochets off walls, metal and cars (`BUL`, `RICO`), glass shatters and slows rounds, furniture and doors take damage. Rounds fly at 3200 px/s (everyone's rifle), crossing the screen in about 0.1 s. Drag and thresholds (`BUL.vMin`, `RICO.minV`) are scaled so range and damage falloff stay as before.
- Tracers (`TRACER`, `tracers`, `stepTraced`): each frame a round leaves a thin streak from where it was to where it got to (the hit point if it struck something), brighter at the head, fading over `TRACER.life` with a faint glow. Yours are warm white, enemy rounds slightly orange.
- Walls are indestructible (no per-cell wall code anymore) but show damage (`wallHit`, `markWall`): a round leaves a pock-mark crater in the wall's own material (`MAT[wallMat[k]].exposed`; cracks on concrete and metal) just inside the face, painted into the floor art and clipped to the tile, throws chips and dust back out, and stops, or ricochets off concrete and metal (`RICO`). `wallMat` from `classifyWalls`: street-facing `#` and columns `C` concrete, elevators `E` metal, other `#` drywall. Light reaches `FACE_LIT` px into wall faces (`lightCast`), so the marks show where the wall is lit or in view. `MAT` holds each material's mark colour, chip colours and whether it ricochets.
- Penetration (`PEN`, `passThrough`): furniture (`FURN[c].mat`), doors and crates (wood), and built-ins (`FIXED_MAT`) may let a round through, slower: soft ~95%, wood ~85%, ceramic/thin metal ~40-50%, metal ~12%. Cover only protects if it's solid.
- Chunks (`launchChunk`, `chunkPts`, `CHUNK`): broken-off fragments are `poly` debris that pop up (`z`, `vz`), tumble, land, skid and bounce off walls, then settle into the floor art like all debris.
- Furniture: each hit bites a jagged piece out where it struck (edge chip or hole, `f.bites`, cap `BITE_MAX`) and launches it. `drawPieces` clips bites out (one clip per bite) and outlines the raw edges. When destroyed, the rest splits into a grid of chunks.
- Windows: the pane is its own object, a thin sheet on the centre line of its tile along the wall (`paneAt`, `paneIntact`, `paneReach`, `paneCross`); the rest of the tile is the window opening over the sill. Bodies can't enter the tile, but rounds, the barrel, smoke and falling glass meet only the pane itself, and only where there's still glass.
  - Windows break in `GLASS_SEG` strips per tile (`glassSeg`, `glassStrip`, `paintGlassGap`): a round meets the pane on its line (`tileEvent`, also from a muzzle already inside the opening) and knocks out the strip it crosses (sometimes neighbours). Rounds pass freely through missing strips, and the tile turns `g` once all are gone.
  - A broken strip shows the bare frame with a jagged break where glass is left beside it, a sliver or two still stuck in the channel, cracks running on into the glass that's left and fine glass dust on the sill (all random, so no two look alike). Its glass falls out as chunks from random points along it: most carried on with the round, some back, some just dropping onto the sill. Debris inside a window tile moves freely in the opening (`airWall`), so the pieces scatter over the sill and the floor beyond instead of sticking to the pane line. Smoke uses `airWall` too: it drifts out of a broken window but not through glass.
- Doors break into planks; crates lose slat bites per hit and fall apart into slats.
- Sound (for gameplay: who hears what): 96-ray extent per source, walls/doors/furniture muffle it. Footsteps reach `WALK_NOISE_MIN`–`WALK_NOISE_MAX` = 40–180 px (crawl to run), gunshots `GUN_NOISE` = 480. Only bots within `SQUAD.radio` = 450 px of a sighting get called in. Up to 2 bots investigate a loud sound; faint ones just make them look. Sound rings are drawn only in the visual-sound mode (`soundVis()`: while the game is muted), so normally you play by ear; bots' hearing works the same either way. In that mode enemy sounds only show if they reach you. Enemy footsteps show as red step rings, at most twice a second per bot (`b.ringT`). You pick them out from `RINGS.hear` (1.6×) a step's sound reach; bots' hearing is unchanged. Enemy rings are the same line as yours (`RINGS.width`), redder, with a pulse dot at the source. Noises from things being knocked about (glass breaking, doors, shoved furniture) go through `noiseBy(who, ...)`: yours and the ally's show as your kind of ring (bots hear both), a bystander's as a faint neutral ring (`nwalk`, nobody reacts), only an enemy's as a red ring with a dot.

## Sound
- Sound is for listening: nothing on screen shows sounds (rings, heard-gunfire edge markers) unless the game is muted (`soundVis`), which doubles as a visual-sound mode for playing without audio (a separate setting can come later).
- Every sound is a real recording embedded in the page (`SFX_MP3`: base64 mono MP3s, 32 kHz 48 kbps for effects, 44.1 kHz 64 kbps for gunshots; `IR_MP3`: room impulse responses). No external files, so the page stays offline. Decoded on first audio init (`sndLoad`, `sndDecode`) into `sndBufs`, a few ms of unpacking per slice (gunshots first) so the first tap doesn't hitch; until then a sound just doesn't play. Audio starts on the first tap/key (browser rule); M or the speaker button top-right mutes (`sndMute`, saved in localStorage as `ds_mute`).
- `sndAt(x, y, loud, reach, dur, verb)` makes a voice relative to you: quieter and duller with distance, arriving late by its travel time (`SND.c`, speed of sound in px/s), panned by its angle from where you face. `sndPlay(v, key, g, rate, dt, lp)` plays a random take of `key` into it (never the same take twice running), re-pitched by up to `SND.pitch`, at `SND.lv[key]`, through a low shelf (`SND.eq.lo` Hz: `SND.bass` per kind + the space's `lo`) and a high shelf (`SND.eq.hi` Hz: the space's `hi`), each tilted at random by `SND.vary` along with the level, so no two plays sound alike. Impacts listed in `SND.layer.keys` get a second, lower, slightly later take under them with chance `SND.layer.p`. A compressor keeps firefights from clipping; `maxVoices` caps overlapping sounds.
- Spaces (`envAt`, cached per tile per map): `out` (floor in `OUTDOOR`); in generated homes by room type (`soft` bedrooms, office, storage; `living` living and dining; `tile` bath, laundry, kitchen; `small` hallways; `garage`); elsewhere `small` or `hall` by 16 rays' average reach, and `tile` for small restroom/kitchen spaces (floor `u`/`k`/`n`). Each space has a convolver (`sndVerbs`, with a lowpass at `SND.room[k].cut`) fed a recorded impulse response (`ir`, default its own name), its tail cut down to about `rt` s for smaller, softer rooms (`irShape`), and a character in `SND.room`: `dry` (outdoors quieter with nothing to build the sound up, small and tiled rooms louder), `verb` (how much it rings), tone `lo`/`hi` (shelf dB: outdoors crisp, small rooms boomy and boxy, tiled rooms bright, carpeted rooms dull, halls warm) and `shot` (which gunshot takes: `in`, recorded in big hard rooms and carrying that room, for hallways, tile, garage and halls; `out`, the dry takes with the room's own ring added in full, for bedrooms and living rooms, so a shot there sounds like that room). A sound rings in the space it happens in, more of it the further off it is, and also in the space you're in if that's a different one (`SND.here`).
- Walls (`sndRoute`, cached per tile pair for 0.5 s): with no clear line, a floor path round through a doorway that's not much longer than straight (`SND.around`) is heard round the corner (somewhat muffled, panned from the opening you can see, delayed by the longer path); otherwise it's heard through the wall (`SND.wall`, `wallsBetween`: each wall on the line makes it `g` quieter and duller from `cut`, a shut door or window less so (`door`, `doorCut`), through a steep two-stage lowpass; the source room's ring comes through more (`verb`), and a gunshot is more thump (`boom`) than crack). Indoors, every shot also gets two early reflections off the walls (`SND.refl`, `roomAt`: the room's average and longest reach by 16 rays, cached per tile), arriving after the round trip at the speed of sound, louder and brighter off hard floors (tile, marble, concrete, wood: `HARD_FLOOR`) and duller and quieter off carpet. Outdoors (where the shot is or where you are) a shot also slaps back off the houses around a beat later (`SND.echo`, a distant take).
- Sounds: gunshots (`sfxShot`, from `shoot`, `pistolShoot`, `botShoot`: `ak_out`/`glock_out` takes when fired outdoors, `ak_in`/`glock_in` (recorded indoors, so they carry their own room and send only `SND.inVerb` to the reverb) inside; a slowed, lowpassed copy of the same take underneath for weight (`SND.boom`, heavier indoors); crossfading into the `far` takes from `SND.far.from` to `full` px), impacts (`sfxHit` by `impactFx` kind: `spark`, `helmet`, `plate`, `drywall`, `concrete`, `wood`, `soft`, `glass`; `flesh` from `bleedHit`), ricochets (`sfxRico`), reloads (`sfxReload` along the reload's progress by `SND.reload`: AK mag out, mag in, charging handle; Glock mag out, mag in, slide; for you, bots and the ally, heard within `reach`) with the old mag clattering on the floor a beat later (`sfxMagDrop`), casings (`sfxCase` on each of the first `SND.cases.hops` bounces, at most one every `gap` s: brass on tile/concrete/street and walls, a knock on wood and furniture, muffled on carpet; 9 mm higher), doors (`sfxDoor`: latch click and often a creak or squeak when the latch gives, which follows the leaf (`creakStep`, `creakStop`, `l.cv`: louder as it swings faster, up to `SND.door.creakW`, faded out and stopped once it stops or bangs, so it's never longer than the movement), a bang when a leaf that swung past `SND.door.shutAt` comes back into the frame or slams into its stop, by speed, with a per-leaf cooldown), near misses (`sfxWhiz`: a round passing within `SND.whiz.r` of you, once per round: a real supersonic flyby for AK rounds, a flutter for 9 mm), footsteps (`sfxStep`, by floor via `STEP_SURF`: `st_soft` carpet, `st_grass` lawn, `st_wood`, `st_grit` concrete/street, `st_hard` tile/marble, plus a crunch wherever glass pieces have settled on the floor (`st_glass`: `glassMap` counts glass debris per 12px patch as it settles, `addGlass`/`glassAt`; your and the ally's steps there also carry `GLASS_STEP` further for enemy hearing); there's no kit or clothing sound (a metal rattle and then a uniform rustle were tried and removed: their recordings clinked): yours on every stride, enemies' as far as you'd pick out their step (same reach as their rings), the ally's within 500 px. Levels in `SND.lv` are measured in the browser with the game loop stopped (otherwise the ally's footsteps leak into the measurement): impacts match the old synthesized mix, footsteps sit about 14 dB under an AK at the same spot (tile/marble > wood > street > carpet), and your own steps play at `SND.ownStep` of someone else's. ## Impact effects
`impactFx(kind, x, y, nx, ny)` (`IMPACT`), all in the sparks list drawn over the darkness:
- `spark` (metal, concrete ricochets, cars, counters, metal furniture): streak sparks plus a brief real flash of light (`flashes` take their own `r`, `k`, `col`, `t0`).
- `drywall` / `concrete`: a dust plume (smoke puffs), concrete with a few sparks.
- `plate` (ceramic plates, ceramic furniture): white powder puff, fragments, a shock ring. `helmet`: sparks, flash and a ring.
- `glass`: twinkling glints (`glint`) and fine dust. `wood` / `soft`: wood dust or fabric fluff.

## Blood
- `splat` (on a wound): lobed splash with a darker rim, spines thrown forward along the shot ending in droplets, and a fan of fine mist (`BLOOD_MIST`).
- Flung drops remember launch speed and direction (`v0`, `a0` from `spray`). `stampBlood` lands them via `bloodDrop`: fast ones stretch into teardrops with a tail and satellite droplet, slow drips land round.
- Pools (`addPool`, `drawPool`) have lobed edges (`q.lobes`), a dark drying rim and a wet sheen until they're stamped (`dry`).
- Blood FX (`BLOODFX`, `bloodFlight`):
  - Droplets fly in an arc (`z`, `vz`), drawn stretched along their path with a wet glint and a floor shadow.
  - They land where they come down: on the floor, on furniture, or on a wall.
  - Floor landings vary by impact (`stampBlood`): a steep, slow drop lands round with a crown of spines and satellites (`bloodCrown`); a fast one leaves a teardrop with a tail (`bloodDrop`); a big one an irregular splash with beads flung ahead (`bloodBlob`).
  - Built-ins (counters, desks, cars) take it in the floor art. Movable furniture keeps its own stains (`stainPiece`, `f.blood`, drawn clipped to the piece in `drawPieces`), so they move with it and go when it breaks.
  - Walls: `wallBlood` paints inside the face, clipped to the tile like bullet marks, stretched along the face by how glancing the hit was, with satellites and short runs.
  - A killing round sprays the wall behind the target if it's within `wallReach` (`exitSpray`): a fan of drops plus a dense splash where the shot's line meets the wall.
  - A red mist hangs at the wound for a moment (`bloodMist`, `mists`, drawn in the scene pass under the darkness).
  - A falling body smears the floor as it slides to a stop.

## Gun smoke
`gunSmoke` emits per shot: a blast that jets out along the bore and stalls into a billow (each puff has its own `drag`, easing down to the drift), a slower cloud at the muzzle, a slant-brake puff to the right and an ejection-port wisp. The muzzle flash (`drawMuzzleFlash`, drawn in the rifle's frame) is different every shot: glow, ragged flame petals, brake-port jets, white-hot core.
- Casings (`ejectCasing`, `stepCasing`, `CASE`): fly in a real arc with height (`z`, `vz`), tumbling end over end (`tb`) over a floor shadow. They bounce with a glint (`tink`), ricochet off walls, furniture sides and people (not the shooter as they leave the port), can land on furniture and low built-ins: cars, counters, desks, sofas, sills (`caseFloor`). They bounce off tall things: walls, columns, crates, shut doors (`caseTall`) and glass still in a window pane (`paneCross`); window tiles are sills they can land on. A casing that starts inside something isn't pinned, and one ejected against a wall starts on the shooter's side, then roll on their side in a curve and get painted in when they stop. Each shot adds barrel `heat`; a hot barrel trails wisps (`barrelWisps`). Smoke stops at walls and is lit by the light map.

## Suppression
- `SUPP` constants; `c.supp` from 0 to 1. Built up by near misses (`whiz` in `stepBullet`, within 55px), impacts nearby (`suppressAt`), hits and cover jolts. It decays, faster in cover (`easeSupp`).
- Tunnel vision under fire (`TUNNEL`, `tunnelHit`, `tunnelStep`, `p.tun`): separate from `supp`, it builds almost at once when an enemy round cracks past within `TUNNEL.near`, lands near you or hits you (eased in at `attack`), holds `hold` s after the last one, then fades. The post-effect below uses the larger of `supp` and `tun`.
- Effects: shakier arms, wider cone. Bots also flinch instead of firing and stop advancing when pinned. The player's view gets tunnel vision (`SUPP_FX`). A smooth blur is built by halving the frame into `postLv` canvases (1/2, 1/4, 1/8, bilinear), then scaling back up; no `ctx.filter`, so it looks the same on iOS. It's masked to the edges so a spot ahead of you stays sharp, and it shrinks as stress rises. A darkening vignette pulses with a heartbeat. The look's strength follows `sqrt(supp)` and the vignette goes near black at the screen edge (`SUPP_FX.vig`/`vigIn`/`vigOut`), so it still reads on the dark, flashlight-free screen. Camera jitter is small (`SUPP_FX.shake`).

## Bots
States patrol → search → attack. BFS pathfinding with occupancy, stuck recovery, hearing. Patrol waypoints must be reachable round heavy furniture (`pickWaypoint` uses `navBlocked`). `followPath` counts a waypoint it gets no closer to for 1.5 s (`wpD`/`wpT`) as blocked, and a bot wedged against light furniture that can't move slips past it (`pushPieces`). They follow the same rules as the player: arms, cover, shoulder switching, magazines (30 rounds, 2s reload, see Magazines), suppression, protection.
- Personalities (`PERSONA`, `rollPersona`, `b.persona`/`b.pers`, read through `PZ(b)`; the ally has none, `NO_PERS`): each enemy rolls veteran, rusher, cautious, green or marksman, which scales shared rules: `aim` (aim wander `BOT.aimNoise` and shot scatter), `react` (`BOT.react`), `nerve` (flinch-instead-of-fire chance and the suppression level that sends a peeker back into cover), `hide`/`peek` (`coverCycle` times), `run` (added to `ROUTE.maxRun`), `burst` (added to `BOT.burst`), `speed` (`followPath`). Rushers are first pick to flank (`flank`); the cautious lie in wait (`ambush` role) instead of trading shots when they can't see you.
- Enemy rifle kit (`PERSONA[].kit`, `rollKit`, `b.att.ak`, `bKit`, `cSupp`): each enemy's AKM gets attachments by chance per personality (veterans kitted out, green conscripts mostly stock, never the IR laser), drawn on his rifle and ragdoll, and working like yours: a grip steadies his aim (×0.8 wander), a red dot adds `ATTFX.dot.ak` to his firing range, a can kills his flash (no muzzle-flash reveal) and his shots are quieter (`aks`, `ATTFX.supp.noise` for hearing), and a visible laser shows as a red beam and dot while he's alert (`drawBotLasers`), giving him away in the dark.
- Posted guards (`b.post`, `postLook`, homes only, chance `post` per personality): instead of patrolling they hold the room they started in, turning between the doors in sight within 9 tiles. They still hear, investigate and fight, and go back to their post afterwards; one that can't path back patrols instead.
- Squad tactics (`SQUAD`, `squadThink`, `tactic`): whoever sees you sets `intel`. The nearest `SQUAD.team` bots get pulled in and given roles, re-thought every 0.5 s:
  - `suppress`: holds ground; without sight it keeps bursting into your last spot for `suppressFor` s.
  - `flank`: up to 2 go quietly (`findFlank`: off your side or behind relative to the suppressor, with a line to you, preferring spots outside your aim).
  - `cover`: hide/peek cycle via `findCover` (a hidden tile with a neighbouring peek tile), reloading in cover and ducking back when suppressed.
  - `ambush`: no fresh contact → hide out of your sight with the light off (`b.dark`) and wait.
  A lone bot uses cover. Roles clear when intel is older than `SQUAD.fresh`. Shooting goes through `botTryFire`.
- Staying out of the open:
  - Tactical moves use `safePath`: Dijkstra where tiles you can see from `intel` (`expo`, `computeExposure`) cost `SQUAD.expoPen` extra. Covered routes don't cut corners (`path.safe`).
  - Every engaged role fights through `coverCycle` (the suppressor with longer peeks; flankers switch to cover once they see you or finish waiting).
  - `findCover` accepts walls (hidden tile plus a peek tile) or low cover (`lowCoverToward`: sturdy furniture, counters, cars or sills between them and you; they press up against it and fire over it), then searches wider (`wideR`).
  - With no cover at all, `findShadow` picks a firing spot off to the side of your aim and repositions when your beam finds them.
  - Out of range, they bound cover to cover (`advanceByCover`) instead of walking at you, or hold if there's nowhere better.
- Careful, not careless (anywhere, inside or out):
  - Cover spots must hide the whole body (`bodyHidden`: centre and both shoulders out of your line). No peek lean while in the `hide` phase.
  - Shadow spots must be dark (`litByScene`); flank spots need low cover or darkness.
  - `routeOk` (`ROUTE`): a route crossing more than `maxRun` tiles you can see and that are lit is refused (unless you're pinned, `p.supp >= pinned`); the bot waits and retries.
  - `noCover`: with nowhere to go it fires if it sees you, else backs off to the nearest tile hidden from you. It never just walks at you.
  - Noises and searches in the street (`OUTDOOR` = 's','a','R', `isOut`, `insideNear`) send an indoor bot only to the nearest indoor spot first (`investigate`).
- Lost contact (`SWEEP`, `sweepThink`, `sweepStep`, `sweep`): after `SWEEP.lost` s without seeing you, the team (whoever had a role, or the nearest bot) gets role `sweep`.
  - Rearm: anyone with plates below `needPlate`, no helmet or under `needAmmo` rounds goes for the best loot within `lootR` (`botGain`, `botTake`: same upgrade/swap rules as yours) and tops up the rifle; the team waits up to `rearmT`.
  - Sweep: rooms (`computeRooms`: connected open floor of one floor code, scraps under `minRoom` merged into a neighbour) are cleared starting from the one you vanished in, then outward, up to `SWEEP.rooms`. Per room (`nextRoom`): stack at the doorway nearest the team, enter one after another (`gap`), each takes a far-apart point (farthest-point spread), scans for `hold` s.
  - Any sighting hands back to combat roles. After the last room (or `maxT`) intel is dropped and they patrol.
- Out of ammo (rifle and vest empty, `dryBot`, `DRY`): they stop fighting and run for the best magazine on the floor, scoring ones near you lower. With none left, they hide out of your sight (`hideSpot`) and watch the way you'd come. Once rearmed, they head back to where they last saw you.
